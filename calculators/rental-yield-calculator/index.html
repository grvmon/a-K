How Is Rental Yield Calculated?

Rental yield measures the annual rental income generated by a property relative to its purchase value. It is expressed as a percentage and serves as a fundamental benchmark for real estate investors across India.

Rental yield is calculated using two primary formulas:

Rental Yield Formula Breakdown

1
Step 1 — Annual Rental Income Annual Rental Income = Monthly Rent × 12
2
Step 2 — Gross Rental Yield Gross Rental Yield% = ( Annual Rental Income / Property Purchase Price ) × 100
3
Step 3 — Net Rental Income & Net Yield Annual Expenses = Annual Maintenance + Other Recurring Costs
Net Rental Income = Annual Rental Income − Annual Expenses
Net Rental Yield% = ( Net Rental Income / Property Purchase Price ) × 100
4
Step 4 — Monthly Rent Required for Target Gross Yield Monthly Rent Required = ( Property Purchase Price × Target Yield% ) / 12

Why Net Rental Yield Matters More Than Gross Yield

While developers and listings often highlight Gross Rental Yield, experienced investors rely on Net Rental Yield. Recurring expenses such as society maintenance charges, property management fees, insurance, and municipal property taxes directly reduce your annual cash flow.

  • Residential Rental Yields in India: Typically range between 2.5% and 4.5% gross depending on the city, micro-market, and luxury segment.
  • Commercial Rental Yields: Typically range between 6.0% and 9.0% gross for Grade-A office spaces and retail shops.

Rental Yield Calculator FAQs

What is a good residential rental yield in India?
In major Indian metro cities (Mumbai, Delhi-NCR, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune), average residential gross rental yield ranges between 2.5% and 4.0%. A net rental yield above 3.2% after maintenance is considered strong for residential real estate.
Does rental yield include property price appreciation?
No. Rental yield measures purely current cash flow return generated by rent. Total investment return (ROI) combines both rental yield and long-term capital appreciation.
How does vacancy impact my effective rental yield?
If a property remains vacant for 1 month every year, your effective annual rent drops by 8.33%, directly reducing both your gross and net rental yield.
Why does the target rent formula use gross yield instead of net yield?
Target gross yield provides a standardized market pricing benchmark based on property value before variable operational expenses are factored in.
