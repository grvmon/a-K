What Does It Cost to Build a House?

The contractor's quoted rate per sq. ft. is only the starting point. Professional fees, structural modifications, finishing choices, and a mandatory contingency buffer determine your true all-in construction budget.

Cost 1
Base Civil Construction
Core foundation, RCC columns, beams, brickwork, and plastering calculated per sq. ft.
Cost 2
Finishing & Fixtures
Flooring tiles, electrical wiring, sanitaryware, doors, windows, painting, and kitchen counters.
Cost 3
Professional & Permit Fees
Architect fees, structural engineer design charges, soil testing, and municipal plan sanction fees.
Cost 4
Contingency Reserve
A 10% reserve buffer to absorb steel/cement price inflation, design modifications, and unexpected site conditions.

Before Starting Construction Checklist

  • ✓ Obtain a detailed itemized Schedule of Quantities (BOQ) from your architect before signing contractor contracts.
  • ✓ Verify whether compound wall, borewell, underground sump, and electrical meter connections are included in contractor scope.
  • ✓ Confirm steel brand (TMT 550D), cement grade (53 Grade OPC/PPC), and sand specification (M-sand vs P-sand).
  • ✓ Ensure plan sanction approval from BBMP / BDA / local municipal authority is obtained prior to excavation.
  • ✓ Maintain a 10% cash contingency reserve strictly earmarked for unbudgeted expenses.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between Base Rate and Effective Construction Cost per sq. ft.?

Base Rate is the raw contractor quote per sq. ft. Effective Construction Cost per sq. ft. includes all additional component upgrades, architect fees, plan approval fees, and contingency reserves divided by total built-up area.

Why is a contingency reserve important in home construction?

A contingency reserve (typically 10%) absorbs material price inflation, structural modifications, and unforeseen site conditions without stalling construction.

How do I prevent double-counting component costs in the calculator?

Select 'Yes — Already Included' if your contractor's quote includes civil, electrical, plumbing, and finishing work.
