info
Important Compound Growth Disclaimer: CAGR assumes a smooth, constant annualized growth rate across the entire holding period. Real investment returns (especially real estate and equities) fluctuate year to year and rarely compound linearly. For investments with ongoing periodic cash flows (SIPs or EMIs), consider IRR/XIRR metrics.

What Is CAGR and Why Does It Matter?

CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate) measures the constant annualized rate at which an investment grows from its starting value to its ending value over a specified period. It provides a clean, standardized annualized percentage that allows investors to compare assets held across different time horizons.

CAGR vs. Total Return: Understanding the Difference

A common mistake is confusing total percentage return with annual return. For example, if a property purchased for ₹10 Lakhs is sold for ₹20 Lakhs after 5 years, the total return is 100%. However, the annualized CAGR is 14.87% p.a., not 20% p.a., because compounding reinvests accumulated gains each year.

CAGR Formula and Calculation Architecture

The standard compound growth formula is:

CAGR = (Ending Value / Starting Value)^(1 / Years) − 1

In reverse mode (calculating future value):

Future Value = Starting Value × (1 + CAGR)^Years

Frequently Asked Questions

Can CAGR be negative?

Yes. If your ending asset value is lower than your starting investment, the resulting CAGR will be negative, representing annualized wealth contraction.

Is CAGR suitable for monthly SIPs or Home Loan EMIs?

No. CAGR assumes a single upfront investment and a single exit. For recurring monthly cash flows, internal rate of return (IRR / XIRR) is required.
