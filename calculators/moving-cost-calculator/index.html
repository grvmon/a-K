How do you use the moving cost calculator?

  1. Enter the amounts from the mover’s quote: truck and transport, packing and materials, labour, loading with any lift or stair charges, and transit insurance.
  2. Add your budget if you have one. GST, storage, society move-in and move-out fees and a buffer are under “Add GST, storage, society fees and buffer”.
  3. Read the total and how it compares with your budget.

What goes into the cost of a move?

The mover’s quote is the main part: a truck, packing material and the people to pack, carry and load. Lifts that cannot be used, stairs and long walks to the truck often cost extra, and transit insurance protects against damage on the way. If the new flat is not ready, storage adds a monthly charge.

GST depends on what you buy. Transport alone for a family’s household goods can be exempt, while a full packing and moving service is usually billed at 18%. Check the line on the quote and pick the matching option. Some societies also charge a fee when you move in or out.

Worked example: a move within the city

A quote of ₹ 10,000 for the truck, ₹ 7,000 for packing, ₹ 12,000 for labour, ₹ 5,000 for loading and lift charges and ₹ 1,500 for insurance comes to ₹ 35,500. With a 5% buffer the move costs ₹ 37,275, leaving ₹ 7,725 of a ₹ 45,000 budget.

Worked example: GST added and two months of storage

If the same quote leaves out 18% GST, the total rises to ₹ 43,985. Without GST but with two months of storage at ₹ 5,000 and ₹ 5,000 of society fees, it is ₹ 53,025, over the budget by ₹ 8,025.

What should you keep in mind?

  • Get two or three written quotes, and check that each lists the same items, so you compare like with like.
  • Ask both societies about move-in and move-out rules, lift booking and any fee or deposit.
  • Read the insurance terms: what is covered, the declared value and how to claim for damage.

Frequently asked questions

It depends on how much you own, the distance and the floors at each end, so get quotes. A local move with ₹ 10,000 for the truck, ₹ 7,000 for packing, ₹ 12,000 for labour, ₹ 5,000 for handling and ₹ 1,500 for insurance costs ₹ 37,275 with a 5% buffer.

It depends on the service. Transport alone of a family’s household goods by a goods transport agency can be exempt, while a full service of packing, loading, transport and unloading is usually billed at 18%. Look at the GST line on the quote, and ask your Chartered Accountant if the rate seems wrong.

For valuable furniture, electronics or a long move, it usually is, because the mover’s own liability is often limited. Check what is covered, whether the cover is on declared value, and how to claim. Note fragile items and photograph them before packing so a claim is easier.

Common extras are lift charges or stair carrying when a lift cannot be used, a long walk from the door to the truck, dismantling and fitting furniture, and waiting time. Ask the mover to list these in the quote so they are not added on the day.

Some do. A society may charge a move-in or move-out fee, ask for a refundable deposit against damage to lifts and lobbies, or allow moves only at set hours. Ask both the old and new society early, and book the service lift.

Sell or give away what you do not need before packing, pack books, clothes and kitchen items yourself, and get at least two quotes. Moving straight into the new flat avoids storage. Keep a small buffer anyway, since quotes are often revised on the day for extra items.
