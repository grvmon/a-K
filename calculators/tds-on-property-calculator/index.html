What Is TDS on Property Purchase?

Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) on property transactions is a withholding statutory mechanism under Indian income tax law. When buying immovable property above the specified statutory threshold from a resident seller, the buyer is required to deduct TDS from the sale consideration price and deposit it directly with the Income Tax Department on behalf of the seller.

Is TDS an Additional Cost for the Property Buyer?

No. TDS does NOT increase your agreed property purchase price. TDS changes how the payment is routed, not the total consideration amount.

Why Is the Stamp Duty Value Required?

Under tax rules, the applicable TDS base is determined as the higher of the Sale Consideration Price OR the Stamp Duty Value (government circle rate). If the stamp duty authority assesses the property value higher than the contract price, TDS must be calculated on the higher stamp duty value to comply with statutory provisions.

Date-Aware Statutory Framework: Section 194-IA vs Section 393

Indian tax law introduced a revised framework for property TDS under the Income Tax Act, 2025:

  • Transactions from 1 April 2026 Onwards: Governed by Section 393 of the Income Tax Act, 2025 using Form 141 (Challan-cum-Statement).
  • Transactions Prior to 1 April 2026: Governed by historical Section 194-IA of the Income Tax Act, 1961 using Form 26QB.

Resident vs. Non-Resident Seller Rules

It is vital to verify the residential status of the seller before executing a property transaction:

  • Resident Seller: Subject to the ₹50 Lakh threshold under Section 393 / Section 194-IA at the standard 1% TDS rate (or 20% if PAN is missing). TAN registration is not required for individual buyers.
  • Non-Resident Seller (NRI): Subject to Section 195 rules. The ₹50 Lakh threshold does NOT apply. TDS must be deducted regardless of property value at higher capital gains tax rates, and the buyer must acquire a Tax Deduction Account Number (TAN).

5-Step Buyer TDS Compliance Workflow

Step 01
Verify Seller PAN & Residency

Verify the seller's PAN card and confirm their residential tax status before making any payment or advance.

Step 02
Calculate TDS Base

Compare Sale Consideration Price against Stamp Duty Value to establish the higher applicable TDS base.

Step 03
Deduct TDS at Payment

Deduct the applicable TDS amount (1%) at the time of credit or payment (including token/advance payments).

Step 04
Deposit & File Form 141 / 26QB

Deposit the withheld TDS online within 30 days from the end of the month of deduction using Form 141 (or Form 26QB).

Step 05
Download & Issue TDS Certificate

Download Form 16B / statutory TDS certificate from TRACES and furnish it to the seller for their tax credit records.

Frequently Asked Questions

What happens if I fail to deduct or deposit TDS on a property purchase?

Failure to deduct or deposit TDS attracts penal interest (1% to 1.5% per month), late filing fee under Section 234E (₹200 per day), and potential penalty under Section 271H. Always complete filing within 30 days of the month of payment.

How is TDS calculated for joint buyers or joint sellers?

For transactions involving multiple buyers or sellers, the total property consideration / stamp duty value is evaluated against the statutory ₹50 Lakh threshold. If applicable, TDS is deducted proportionately based on ownership share, and separate challan statements are filed for each buyer-seller combination.

Do I need a TAN (Tax Deduction Account Number) to pay TDS on property?

No. For buying property from a resident seller, individual buyers do not need a TAN. The payment and filing are completed using your PAN on the official Income Tax portal.
