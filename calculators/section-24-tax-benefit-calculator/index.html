How do you use the Section 24 tax benefit calculator?

  1. Enter the interest you will pay this year (your lender’s certificate shows it), and say if the home is lived in or let out.
  2. Enter your other taxable income, and any interest you paid before possession. For a let-out home, add the rent.
  3. Read how much interest you can deduct and the tax it saves. Loan purpose, the five-year completion rule and property tax are under “Adjust loan purpose, completion and property tax”.

What does Section 24 allow now?

Section 24(b) of the 1961 Act is now section 22 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, in force from 1 April 2026. It lets you deduct interest on money borrowed to buy, build, repair or renew a house from your income from house property.

  • Home you live in: up to ₹ 2 lakh a year in total across such homes, if the loan was for buying or building and the work was completed within five years from the end of the year you borrowed. Otherwise, and for repair loans, the limit is ₹ 30,000. Old regime only.
  • Let-out home: no cap on the interest, but a loss from house property can be set off against salary or other income only up to ₹ 2 lakh a year in the old regime. The rest carries forward for up to eight years, against house property income only. The new regime allows no set-off at all.
  • Interest before possession: the total is claimed in five equal parts, starting the year the home is completed. On a home you live in, these parts sit inside the ₹ 2 lakh cap.

Worked example: ₹ 2.5 lakh interest on a home you live in

With ₹ 12 lakh of other taxable income, ₹ 2 lakh is deductible and the other ₹ 50,000 is lost. The deduction saves ₹ 62,400 in the old regime. If the home had not been completed within five years, only ₹ 30,000 would count, saving ₹ 9,360.

Worked example: pre-construction interest, and a let-out flat

A buyer who paid ₹ 3 lakh of interest before possession and pays ₹ 1.5 lakh this year adds ₹ 60,000, one-fifth, to this year’s figure. The total of ₹ 2,10,000 is still capped at ₹ 2 lakh. Take a flat let out at ₹ 3 lakh a year with ₹ 10,000 of property tax and ₹ 5 lakh of interest: the loss is ₹ 2,97,000. ₹ 2 lakh is set off this year, saving ₹ 1,25,736 in the old regime, and ₹ 97,000 carries forward.

What should you keep in mind?

  • Keep the interest certificate from your lender; the ₹ 2 lakh limit needs one.
  • Joint owners who are co-borrowers each get their own ₹ 2 lakh limit on their share of the interest.
  • Principal repaid is a separate deduction. See the Section 80C tax savings calculator, and compare both regimes with the home loan tax benefit calculator.

Interest paid before the home is completed is added up and claimed in five equal parts, starting with the year of completion. On a home you live in, each part counts inside the ₹ 2 lakh yearly cap along with the current year’s interest. ₹ 3 lakh of such interest adds ₹ 60,000 a year.
