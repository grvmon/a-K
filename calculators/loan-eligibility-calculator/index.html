How do you use the loan eligibility calculator?

  1. Enter your net monthly salary (in hand, after tax and PF) and any EMIs you already pay.
  2. Pick the FOIR your bank uses. 45% is common; some banks allow 50% to 55% for higher salaries.
  3. Add a co-applicant’s salary, the interest rate and the tenure under “Co-applicant, rate and tenure”, then read the loan, the property budget and the cash you need upfront.

How it’s calculated

The calculator works out how much EMI your income can carry, then turns that EMI into a loan amount at your rate and tenure.

How banks decide your home loan eligibility

Bank loan sanction decisions are driven by algorithmic underwriting models evaluating your net disposable income, credit history, career stability, and loan-to-value benchmarks.

1. The FOIR Mechanism

Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio (FOIR) measures the percentage of your monthly salary committed to loan installments. Lenders cap FOIR at 40% to 50% to ensure borrowers retain a comfortable living cushion for household expenses, taxation, and unforeseen emergencies.

2. Loan-to-Value (LTV) Bounds

Under Reserve Bank of India (RBI) prudential guidelines, banks finance up to 90% for loans up to ₹30 Lakh, up to 80% for loans between ₹30 Lakh and ₹75 Lakh, and up to 75% for loans exceeding ₹75 Lakh. The balance must be funded as upfront borrower equity.

3. CIBIL Bureau Benchmarks

A credit score above 750 positions you in the prime borrower band, securing lender benchmark spreads (e.g. 8.35% p.a.). Scores under 700 may incur risk premiums of 50 to 100 bps or require higher down payments to achieve mortgage sanction.

4 Ways to Expand Your Borrowing Capacity

1. Add an Earning Co-Applicant

Including your spouse, working parent, or son combines distinct income streams, increasing your collective FOIR ceiling and raising loan capacity by 40% or more, and up to double.

2. Foreclose High-EMI Short Term Debts

Car loans and personal loans chew up significant monthly FOIR allowance. Every ₹10,000 of active loan EMI cleared makes available ~₹ 11.5 L in new home loan principal.

3. Select a Longer Tenure (25–30 Years)

Extending your repayment tenure from 20 to 30 years lowers the per-lakh monthly installment requirement, instantly qualifying you for a higher initial sanction.

4. Ask About Step-Up Repayment Plans

Specialized bank products (such as SBI FlexiPay or ICICI Step-Up) model annual career salary progression, permitting lower initial EMIs as career earnings expand.

FOIR (Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio) is the percentage of your net monthly income that banks allow for total monthly debt repayments (including all active loans plus your new home loan EMI). Indian lenders typically cap FOIR at 40% to 50% for standard salaries, though high earners (>₹ 2.5 L/month) may be permitted up to 55%–60% due to larger disposable income cushions.

Assuming a standard 45% FOIR cap and zero existing debt, a net monthly salary of ₹ 1.5 L permits a maximum EMI of ₹ 67,500. At an 8.50% interest rate over a 20-year tenure, this yields a maximum loan eligibility of approximately ₹ 77.8 L. Over a 25-year tenure, eligibility increases to ~₹ 83.8 L. Adding a co-applicant earning ₹ 75,000/month raises eligibility to ~₹ 1.17 Cr.

Existing EMIs directly reduce your permissible home loan EMI rupee-for-rupee under lender FOIR limits. For instance, if your net monthly salary allows a ₹ 75,000 maximum debt capacity, but you are currently servicing a ₹ 15,000 car loan EMI, your available home loan EMI capacity drops to ₹ 60,000, slashing your loan eligibility by ~₹ 17.3 L at 8.50% over 20 years.
