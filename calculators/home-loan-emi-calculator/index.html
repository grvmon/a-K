What Your Home Loan EMI Really Costs

Your monthly EMI is only one part of the cost of a home loan. The total amount you repay depends on the loan amount, interest rate, and tenure you choose.

Loan Amount

The amount you borrow after your down payment.

Total Interest

The estimated interest you pay to the lender over the full loan tenure.

Total Repayment

The total amount you repay over the life of the loan, including both principal and interest.

A Lower EMI Doesn't Always Mean a Lower-Cost Loan

Choosing a longer loan tenure can reduce your monthly EMI, but it can significantly increase the total interest you pay over time.

Compare different loan tenures using the calculator above to see how your monthly EMI and total interest change.

The right loan isn't simply the one with the lowest EMI. It's the one that balances your monthly affordability with your total cost of borrowing.

How Is Home Loan EMI Calculated?

Your home loan EMI is calculated using three main factors: loan amount, interest rate, and loan tenure.

The EMI includes both the principal amount you borrowed and the interest charged by the lender. As you repay the loan, the outstanding principal reduces and the interest component changes over time.

The Home Loan EMI Formula

The standard EMI formula is:

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)ⁿ ÷ [(1 + r)ⁿ − 1]

Where:

  • P = Principal loan amount
  • r = Monthly interest rate
  • n = Total number of monthly instalments

For example, a ₹2.68 crore loan at an annual interest rate of 7.12% for 20 years will have a different EMI from the same loan taken at a higher interest rate or for a different tenure.

You don't need to calculate the formula manually. Enter your loan details into the AcreNKey Home Loan EMI Calculator above to instantly estimate your monthly EMI, total interest, and total repayment.

What Determines Your Home Loan EMI?

Loan Amount

The amount you borrow directly affects your EMI. A larger loan generally means a higher monthly repayment.

Interest Rate

A higher interest rate increases both your monthly EMI and the total interest paid over the loan tenure.

Loan Tenure

A longer tenure generally reduces your monthly EMI, but increases the total interest you pay over the life of the loan.

Down Payment

A larger down payment reduces the amount you need to borrow. This can lower both your monthly EMI and total interest, provided the down payment does not leave you without sufficient financial reserves.

Use the calculator above to adjust these variables and see how each change affects your home loan.

How Interest Rate and Loan Tenure Affect Your EMI

Two home loans with the same loan amount can have very different monthly EMIs and total repayment costs depending on the interest rate and loan tenure.

Understanding this trade-off can help you choose a loan structure that fits both your monthly budget and your long-term financial plans.

How Interest Rate Affects Your Home Loan EMI

A higher interest rate generally means a higher monthly EMI and more interest paid over the life of the loan.

Even a small difference in interest rate can have a significant impact when you are repaying a large home loan over 15, 20 or 25 years.

Use the calculator above to compare different interest rates and see how they affect your monthly EMI and total interest.

How Loan Tenure Affects Your Home Loan EMI

Loan tenure is the length of time you take to repay your home loan.

A longer tenure generally reduces your monthly EMI because the repayment is spread over more months. However, you may pay substantially more interest over the full loan period.

A shorter tenure usually means a higher monthly EMI, but a lower total interest cost.

The Trade-Off

Shorter tenure → Higher EMI → Lower total interest

Longer tenure → Lower EMI → Higher total interest

There is no single loan tenure that is right for every buyer. The right choice depends on your income, existing financial commitments, available savings and how much monthly repayment you are comfortable with.
