How do you use the GST on property calculator?

  1. Enter the agreement value before GST and the carpet area of the flat in sq ft.
  2. Say if you are buying under construction, a ready home after its completion certificate, or a resale.
  3. Read the GST, the rate used and the price with GST. If the builder’s quote already includes GST, or part of the price is paid after completion, open “Adjust GST rates and payment details”.

When do you pay GST on a home?

GST applies when you buy a flat that is still under construction from a builder. It does not apply to a ready home where the full price is paid after the completion certificate, or to a resale from another owner. Stamp duty and registration are separate state charges, not GST.

  • 5% without input tax credit on residential flats, from 1 April 2019.
  • 1% without input tax credit on affordable flats: carpet area up to 60 sq m (about 646 sq ft) in a metropolitan city such as Bengaluru, and a price up to ₹ 45 lakh. Both tests must be met.
  • These are effective rates after the deduction for land, so they apply to the full agreement value.

The GST rate changes of September 2025 cut rates on inputs such as cement but left these residential rates unchanged.

Worked example: ₹ 1 crore flat, 1,200 sq ft carpet

The flat is over 60 sq m and over ₹ 45 lakh, so the 5% rate applies: GST is ₹ 5,00,000 and the price with GST is ₹ 1,05,00,000. If ₹ 20 lakh of the price falls due after the completion certificate, GST is charged on ₹ 80 lakh only: ₹ 4,00,000.

Worked example: ₹ 42 lakh flat, 600 sq ft carpet

600 sq ft is 55.7 sq m, under the 60 sq m limit, and the price is under ₹ 45 lakh, so the 1% rate applies: GST is ₹ 42,000. The same flat at ₹ 48 lakh would pay 5%.

What should you keep in mind?

  • Ask the builder for a payment schedule showing which instalments fall before and after the completion certificate.
  • Check that the agreement states the carpet area clearly, since the affordable test uses carpet area, not super built-up area.
  • This page covers flats in residential projects. Commercial units and plots follow other rules, so please check those with your Chartered Accountant.
