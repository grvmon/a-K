Why Luxury Home Affordability Is Different

Acquiring a luxury home involves financial commitments beyond the base property cost. Additional costs like stamp duty, registration fees, interiors, parking, club memberships, and maintenance deposits require substantial upfront liquid reserves.

Step 1
Income & EMI Limit
Calculates your maximum monthly EMI capacity after existing debt commitments using a conservative 40% income cap.
Step 2
Total Acquisition Cost
Adds estimated taxes, registration, brokerage, interiors, and developer deposits (typically 8%) to the base property price.
Step 3
Cash & Liquidity Buffer
Verifies that liquid savings cover the required down payment and additional costs without draining emergency reserves.
Step 4
Binding Constraint Check
Identifies whether monthly income or upfront cash reserves are the main limiting factor on your maximum property price.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is Luxury Home Affordability different from standard home affordability?

Luxury home affordability factors in substantial additional acquisition costs—such as stamp duty, registration, interior design, club memberships, and maintenance deposits—alongside income and liquid post-purchase cash reserves.

How does the calculator determine maximum affordable property price?

The calculator evaluates both your Income-Based Loan Limit and your Cash/Liquidity Capacity. Your maximum affordable property price is constrained by whichever limit is lower.

What do the Luxury Affordability Classifications mean?

Scores of 80-100 indicate AFFORDABLE, 60-79 indicate STRETCH, and 0-59 indicate NOT AFFORDABLE based on conservative debt and liquidity buffers.

What is the default Maximum EMI-to-Income ratio for luxury homes?

The calculator defaults to a conservative 40% EMI-to-income ratio (rather than 50%) to ensure high-net-worth buyers preserve significant disposable cash flow post-purchase.
