How the Reducing-Balance Home Loan EMI is Calculated

In a standard reducing-balance loan, interest is calculated solely on the outstanding principal at the beginning of each calendar month. The standard mathematical formula applied by all scheduled Indian commercial banks is:

EMI = [P × r × (1 + r)ⁿ] ÷ [(1 + r)ⁿ − 1]

Where:

  • P = Principal borrowed (Property Value − Upfront Down Payment)
  • r = Monthly interest rate (Annual Interest Rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100)
  • n = Loan duration expressed in months (Years × 12)

The goal of property financing is not simply securing the lowest monthly payment. It is achieving comfortable monthly cash flow while minimising total interest outgo over the life of the loan.

How Prepaying Your Home Loan Saves Interest: Calculation & Strategies

In the initial 5 years of a 20-year home loan, 70% to 80% of each monthly installment goes toward interest rather than principal reduction. Prepaying early reduces the core balance before compound interest can accrue over the remaining decades. Below is the exact mathematical impact modelled on a ₹80 Lakh home loan at 8.50% interest over a 20-year tenure (Monthly EMI: ₹69,426):

Strategy 1 (One Extra EMI Yearly): Paying one extra EMI (₹ 69,426) once per year reduces your 20-year tenure to approximately 16.75 years (saving 3.25 years) and cuts cumulative interest by approximately ₹ 16.5 L.

Strategy 2 (5% Annual Principal Prepayment): Prepaying 5% of the starting principal (₹ 4 L annually) starting in year 2 reduces the total repayment period from 20 years down to approximately 10.6 years and saves approximately ₹ 43 L in lifetime interest.

Why Year 1–3 Prepayment Saves Up to 13x More: A ₹ 1 L prepayment made in month 12 saves about ₹ 3.89 L in lifetime interest over a 20-year span at 8.50%. That same ₹ 1 L prepayment made in year 17 saves only about ₹ 28,500 in interest because most of the principal balance has already amortised.

Zero Prepayment Penalties: Under RBI prudential regulations, scheduled commercial banks and housing finance companies are prohibited from levying foreclosure charges or prepayment penalties on floating-rate home loans to individual borrowers.

Home Loan Tax Benefits: Section 24(b) and Section 80C Explained

Tax relief on home loans can meaningfully reduce your effective borrowing cost, but its applicability depends strictly on your chosen tax regime:

  • Old Tax Regime Only: Deductions under Section 24(b) (interest) and Section 80C (principal) are only available if you elect the Old Personal Income Tax Regime. Under the default New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC), deductions for self-occupied property interest and principal repayments are completely disallowed.
  • Section 24(b) (Interest Exemption): Under the Old Regime, you can deduct up to ₹ 2,00,000 per financial year against taxable income for a self-occupied property. In the 30% tax slab (+4% cess), this translates to an annual tax saving of ₹ 62,400 (~₹ 5,200/month).
  • Section 80C (Principal Deduction): Up to ₹ 1,50,000 per financial year can be deducted for the principal portion of your EMI repayments (within the combined ₹ 1.5 L 80C umbrella including PF, ELSS, and insurance), saving up to ₹ 46,800 annually.
  • Joint Co-Borrowers & Co-Owners: If spouses or family members jointly own the property and jointly service the loan, both co-borrowers can claim separate deductions in proportion to their EMI contributions, enabling up to ₹ 4,00,000 in combined annual interest deduction and up to ₹ 3,00,000 in annual principal deduction.
  • Income-tax Code Notice & Advisory: Under the Income-tax Act, 2025 (effective 1 April 2026), tax sections and clause mappings are subject to statutory updates alongside existing Sections 24(b) and 80C, sections and clause numbers are subject to statutory renumbering. Individual tax liabilities depend on your total taxable income, deductions claimed, and employer structuring. Always verify your specific tax strategy and regime selection with a qualified Chartered Accountant (CA) or tax advisor before filing.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

What is a home loan EMI and how is it calculated?

A home loan EMI (Equated Monthly Instalment) is a fixed payment amount made by a borrower to a lender at a specified calendar date each month. It is computed using the reducing balance formula: EMI = [P × r × (1 + r)ⁿ] ÷ [(1 + r)ⁿ − 1], where P represents loan principal, r is the monthly interest rate, and n is the total number of monthly instalments.

How much is the EMI for a ₹ 1 Cr home loan?

At a benchmark interest rate of 8.50% per annum, the monthly EMI for a ₹ 1 Cr home loan over a 20-year tenure is ₹ 86,782 per month. Total interest payable over 20 years equals approximately ₹ 1.08 Cr, bringing total repayment to ₹ 2.08 Cr.

What is the minimum monthly salary needed for a ₹ 1 Cr home loan?

Most scheduled Indian commercial banks enforce a Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio (FOIR) cap of 40% to 50%. Since the EMI for a ₹ 1 Cr loan at 8.50% over 20 years is ~₹ 86,782, a borrower typically requires a net monthly in-hand household income of at least ₹ 1,73,600 to ₹ 2,17,000, assuming no other active debt obligations.

How do Section 24(b) and Section 80C reduce effective home loan EMI?

Under the old tax regime, homebuyers can deduct up to ₹ 2 L per financial year against interest paid under Section 24(b) for self-occupied property, saving up to ₹ 62,400 annually in the 30% tax slab (~₹ 5,200/month). Principal repayments qualify for up to ₹ 1.5 L deduction under Section 80C, saving up to ₹ 46,800 annually (~₹ 3,900/month). Under the default New Tax Regime, these deductions are ₹ 0 for self-occupied properties.

Is it better to reduce EMI or tenure when making prepayments?

Reducing the tenure usually saves more interest than reducing the EMI. Interest is charged on the outstanding principal, so each prepayment that shortens the loan removes months of future interest. Keeping the same EMI and cutting the tenure works well if your budget can carry it. Choose a lower EMI instead if you need more monthly cash flow or a bigger safety buffer.

Why does prepaying principal in the first 3 years save significantly more interest?

A ₹ 1 L prepayment made in month 12 saves about ₹ 3.89 L in interest over a 20-year tenure at 8.5%, versus about ₹ 28,500 if prepaid in year 17 (approximately 13 times more interest saved). Early principal reduction cuts the principal balance before years of interest accumulate on it.

Does home loan EMI include property registration and stamp duty?

No. Under RBI regulations, commercial banks are required to exclude statutory charges such as stamp duty and registration fees from property valuation when calculating loan-to-value (LTV) limits. These closing expenses cannot be added to your sanctioned home loan and must be funded separately in cash.

What are the RBI Loan-to-Value (LTV) limits for home loans?

Per RBI prudential guidelines, maximum home loan funding is capped by ticket size: up to 90% LTV for loans up to ₹ 30 L (minimum 10% down payment), up to 80% LTV for loans between ₹ 30 L and ₹ 75 L (minimum 20% down payment), and up to 75% LTV for loans above ₹ 75 L (minimum 25% down payment). Statutory stamp duty and registration fees are excluded from the property cost when calculating these caps.

How do RBI repo rate changes affect floating rate home loans?

Under RBI External Benchmark Linked Rate (EBLR) regulations, commercial banks must reset floating home loan interest rates automatically when the RBI repo rate shifts. Under RBI guidelines, lenders must give borrowers the option to choose between an increased EMI or an elongated tenure at reset, and must provide transparent amortisation statements.
