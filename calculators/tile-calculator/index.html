How Many Tiles Do I Need?

Calculating the exact number of tiles required involves measuring net floor area, adding a cutting wastage buffer, converting tile dimensions, and rounding up to whole boxes.

Step 1
Measure Floor Area
Multiply room length by width (Length × Width) to get the net floor area in square feet or square meters.
Step 2
Add Cutting Wastage
Add 10% wastage for straight layouts or 15% for diagonal patterns to cover edge cuts, trims, and breakage.
Step 3
Divide by Tile Area
Divide the total required area by the area of a single tile to find the exact minimum number of tiles needed.
Step 4
Round Up to Full Boxes
Divide tiles required by tiles per box and round up to the nearest whole box to avoid shortage on site.

Before Ordering Tiles Checklist

  • ✓ Double check room length and width in feet or meters.
  • ✓ Confirm single tile dimensions (e.g., 600×600 mm, 1200×600 mm).
  • ✓ Check how many tiles are packed in a single box (usually 4 tiles for 2×2 ft).
  • ✓ Add 10% wastage for straight laying or 15% for herringbone/diagonal laying.
  • ✓ Keep 1 full extra box of spare tiles for future plumbing or electrical repair work.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many tiles do I need for my room?

To calculate tile count, multiply room length by width to find floor area, add 10% for cutting wastage, and divide by the individual tile area. Always round up to the nearest whole tile and full box.

Why should I include a 10% wastage buffer when buying tiles?

Wastage accounts for edge cuts, corner trims, diagonal layouts, handling breakage, and future tile replacement reserves.

How many tiles are in a standard tile box?

Standard 2x2 ft (600x600 mm) tile boxes usually contain 4 tiles covering 16 sq. ft. Larger 4x2 ft tiles typically come in boxes of 2 or 3 tiles.
