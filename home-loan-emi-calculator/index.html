How is home loan EMI calculated?

Home loan EMI (Equated Monthly Instalment) is calculated using the standard reducing-balance formula:

EMI = P × r × (1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n − 1)

Where P is the principal loan amount (Property Value minus Down Payment), r is the monthly interest rate (Annual Rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100), and n is the total number of monthly instalments (Tenure in Years × 12).

What affects your home loan EMI?

Four primary variables govern your monthly outflow:

  • Loan Amount: Higher borrowing directly increases your monthly EMI.
  • Interest Rate: Even a 0.25% shift in annual rate creates significant compound interest differences over 20 years.
  • Loan Tenure: Longer tenures reduce monthly EMI but substantially increase total interest payable.
  • Down Payment: Paying a higher upfront down payment lowers your principal loan requirement and overall interest burden.

Does a longer loan tenure reduce EMI?

Yes, opting for a 25 or 30-year tenure lowers your monthly payment compared to a 15-year tenure. However, this comes with a tradeoff: you will pay significantly more cumulative interest over the loan duration.

Should you focus only on the EMI?

Prudent homebuyers evaluate total cost of ownership rather than monthly EMI alone. Consider statutory registration costs (approx. 5.6% to 6.6% in Karnataka), builder maintenance deposits, interior costs, interest rate fluctuations on floating loans, and emergency liquidity reserves.
