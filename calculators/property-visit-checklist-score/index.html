How do you use the property visit checklist score?

  1. Take the checklist to the flat. For each of the eight checks, tap Good, OK or Poor based on what you see, or leave it at Not yet.
  2. Read the score, the verdict and any red flag. Only the checks you have done count, and the panel shows how many are left.
  3. Visit again at a different time of day for anything rated Poor or not yet checked.

How is the score worked out?

Each check has a weight: two points for commute, layout, daylight, cracks and seepage, and water; one point for noise, safety and upkeep. Good earns the full weight, OK earns 60% of it and Poor earns nothing. Your result is the points earned divided by the weight of the checks you did, out of 100. The weights are a simple guide, so move them in your head if one matters more to you.

Two checks are red flags. Cracks, seepage or damp can mean costly repairs, and a weak water supply often means paying for tankers every month. A Poor on either one flags the flat, however good the rest is.

What to look for at each check

Things to check in person on a Bengaluru site visit.
CheckWhat to look for
Commute and areaDrive the route to work at peak hour; note the road width, flooding signs and nearby schools and hospitals.
Layout and room sizesMeasure the bedrooms and kitchen; check where furniture goes and whether rooms waste space on passages.
Daylight and airVisit in daylight; check which way the windows face and whether a building blocks them.
NoiseListen with the windows shut and open; look for a main road, metro line or generator nearby.
Cracks, seepage or dampLook at ceilings, bathroom walls and below windows for stains, peeling paint or cracks.
Water supplyAsk the source, how many hours water comes and how often tankers are bought; look at the sump and tanks.
Safety and securityCheck the gate, guards, cameras, fire extinguishers and exits.
Common areas and upkeepLook at the lifts, lobby, basement and garden, and ask for recent maintenance bills.

What should you keep in mind?

  • A site visit cannot tell you about the title, approvals or occupancy certificate. Have your lawyer check those documents.
  • Speak to two or three residents. They will tell you about water, power cuts and the society more honestly than anyone selling the flat.
  • Visit at least twice, once on a weekday evening, before you decide.
