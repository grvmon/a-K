What Drives Real Estate Investment Returns?

A complete property investment analysis balances both ongoing cash flow (net rental yield) and long-term equity growth (capital appreciation), while factoring in mandatory buying and exit transaction costs.

Pillar 1
Net Rental Yield
Calculates real cash flow after deducting recurring maintenance, property taxes, insurance, and management fees from gross rent.
Pillar 2
Capital Appreciation & Compound Growth
Projects compound property appreciation over the selected holding horizon to model total equity gain upon exit.
Pillar 3
Friction & Transaction Costs
Factors in upfront stamp duty/registration fees (buying costs) and brokerage/closing fees (selling costs) to measure net ROI.
Pillar 4
CAGR & Annualized Return
Normalizes total net returns into an annualized compound rate of growth (CAGR) for easy comparison with alternative asset classes.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is the Property Investment Score calculated?

The Property Investment Score (0-100) evaluates 4 key return metrics: Unleveraged ROI (30%), CAGR (30%), Net Rental Yield (20%), and Capital Appreciation Rate (20%) normalized against market benchmarks.

Why does the calculator include buying and selling costs?

Stamp duty, registration, brokerage, and transaction costs reduce net real estate returns. Factoring buying and selling costs prevents inflated return estimates.

What do the Property Investment Ratings mean?

Scores of 80-100 indicate EXCELLENT potential, 65-79 indicate STRONG, 50-64 indicate MODERATE, and 0-49 indicate WEAK investment potential.

Is mortgage financing included in the base Investment Score?

The base Investment Score evaluates unleveraged cash returns. An optional financing panel allows buyers to inspect EMI and leverage cash-flow burdens separately.
